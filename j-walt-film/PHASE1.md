# J-WALT brand film — Phase 1 (direction for approval)

30 s · 1920×1080 · 60 fps · 120 BPM (beat = 0.5 s = 30 frames, bar = 2 s)

**Idea in one line:** a single drawn line runs through the whole film. It becomes a joint in
a real J-WALT wall, the drawing becomes the photographed room, plates are built up from base
lines, facts rise out of rules, and at the end everything simplifies back to one line, and
the J-WALT wordmark rises out of it.

Status of this phase: source analysed, assets catalogued, motion engine built, four
keyframes rendered from the engine at their real timecodes. **No video rendered.**
Scenes marked *(Phase 2)* below are specified but not built yet.

---

## 1. Source and assets (summary; full table in `analysis/catalogue.md`)

- `J-WALT-Brand-Film-Source-Pack.pdf`: 21 pages, each a flattened 2000×1125 JPEG slide.
  27 images extracted: **24 real photographs** (7 of them heavily processed and soft, and
  1 not attributed to any project) and **3 renders** (p11, p12, p17), plus graphics pages
  (logo, services, milestones, process).
- `J-WALT-Brand-Film-Source-Notes.txt`: **not present in this session**. Nothing in this
  plan relies on it.
- Hard constraint found: photos are **≤ ~1,100 px** on the long side, and nearly all are
  square or portrait. A full-bleed 16:9 frame would need ~1.9× enlargement and look soft.
  So the film is designed as an **editorial layout**: photographs sit on grid-aligned
  plates that bleed off one or more frame edges. The engine never enlarges a photo beyond
  **1.25×** (current worst case 1.18×). The design turns the constraint into the look of an
  architecture monograph.

**Primary images (8):** heid-reception, heid-boardroom, heid-stained-corridor,
heid-corridor-glass, heid-curved-glass, corys-openplan-b, omoda-reception,
snoc-boardroom-built. **Secondary (7):** heid2-hall, corys-openplan-a, heid-private-office,
heid-teamwork-joinery, corys-glass-b, snoc-shelving-built, omoda-workspace-b.

## 5. Typography and visual system

- **Typeface:** Inter Tight (SIL OFL, vendored in `assets/fonts`), one family, weights
  300/400/500. Uppercase with measured tracking, like an architecture monograph.
  - Statements and project names: 54 px / 300 / +0.12 em
  - Services: 30 / 300 / +0.10 em · values: 23 / 400 / +0.06 em
  - Labels and indices: 15 / 500 / +0.20 em, graphite
  - End frame: descriptor 20 / 400 / +0.36 em, tagline 20 / 500 / +0.42 em
  - Nothing is below 15 px at 1080p, so text stays readable on a phone.
- **Colour:** paper `#F2F0EB` (warm architectural white), ink `#1D1D1B`, graphite
  `#76736D`, and one accent, J-WALT red `#BE1B20`. The red was sampled from the logo on
  p1 and appears only twice: the datum mark in frame 1 and the wordmark at the end.
  Photographs are not colour-graded.
- **Grid:** 12 columns, 120 px margins, 24 px gutters, 5% action-safe checked by QA. Plates
  snap to columns and bleed off frame edges.
- **Lines:** architectural weight hierarchy. Structure and joinery outlines 1.5 px,
  pendant rings 1.15 px, panel joints 1.0 px. Dimension strings use 45° ticks and
  extension lines, as on a real drawing.
- **Motion:** four curves only: `arch` (0.65,0,0.35,1) for lines, masks and panels;
  `reveal` (0.16,1,0.3,1) for type; `camera` (0.37,0,0.25,1); and linear drift. No
  overshoot or bounce, no particles, glows, flares or blur.
- **Logo:** the wordmark was rebuilt as clean vector geometry from the p1 raster: fitted
  straight edges, true corners and tangent-continuous fillets. It matches the source at
  **IoU 0.984**, and its rendered edges measure 0.86 px (10–90%).

## 7. Technical decisions

- **Engine:** Canvas 2D. `film.seek(t)` repaints each frame from scratch, so frames are a
  pure function of time. It has no hidden state, reads no clock and uses no unseeded
  randomness, so any frame renders identically, in any order.
- **Camera:** `frame(img, aperture, {zoom, cx, cy, px, py})`. Framing is based on "cover";
  the photo always fills its plate, and the camera can never leave a photo's safe view.
  Those views crop out two cardboard boxes, a CCTV dome, a floor mat, an office chair,
  festive décor, flags, a leaflet and a stray logo fragment. Every frame reports its per-photo enlargement for QA.
- **Registration:** the reception line drawing was authored on a coordinate grid and then
  snapped to real edges and joints by edge-response search (`analysis/snap_lines.py`). The
  three pendant rings are RANSAC-fitted ellipses.
- **Type rendering:** the canvas keeps an alpha channel and Chromium runs with
  `--disable-lcd-text`, because LCD subpixel antialiasing colour-fringes type in video. The
  masked-reveal clip includes the descender zone; a clip at the baseline had turned
  "5,600" into "5.600".
- **Render pipeline (Phase 2):** Playwright + Chromium, served over a local static server
  (keeps the canvas same-origin), lossless PNG frames, then FFmpeg libx264 High profile,
  yuv420p, BT.709 tagged, CRF ≈ 12 / preset slow, 60 fps, AAC 320 kb/s 48 kHz stereo,
  `+faststart`. A static FFmpeg build with libx264 is used (`imageio-ffmpeg`), since the
  container has no system FFmpeg.
- **Sound (Phase 2):** there is no licensed music in this environment, so the score will be
  an original piece synthesised in code (deterministic, 120 BPM): pad, sub pulse,
  felt-pluck motif, sparse hats, plus synthesised sound effects mixed to about −14 LUFS
  with true peak ≤ −1 dBTP. If you have a licensed track, I'll cut the film to it instead;
  it would sound more polished than a code-built score.
- **Motion blur:** none planned. All moves are slow; temporal subframes will be tested only
  on the panel slide (T3) and removed if they ghost.

## 8. Uncertainties in the source material (please confirm)

1. **Notes file missing.** `J-WALT-Brand-Film-Source-Notes.txt` was not provided. If it
   holds constraints (approved clients, wording), I have not seen them.
2. **"700,000+" has no label** in the pack. It carries only an arrows-in-a-square icon, so
   possibly square feet. The brief's "REFURBISHMENT & RENOVATION" wording does not appear
   next to it, so the figure is **left out** until you confirm the exact label.
3. **Sectors.** Retail, Residential and F&B do not appear in the pack. Only offices (in the
   photos) and "Warehouse" (p19) are supported, so no sector list is planned.
4. **"Design and build MEP"** is shown in the pack as two separate services, "Design and
   build" and "MEP". The film uses "MEP" alone.
5. **Corys naming.** The page shows the "+GF+" and "CORYS" logos, and its text says "Corys
   Piping Systems". The plan uses CORYS PIPING SYSTEMS and DUBAI INVESTMENT PARK, dropping
   the plot number.
6. **Heidelberg appears twice:** 5,600 SQF / 70 days (p5–8) and 12,647 SQF / 4 months
   (p21, under the Warehouse divider). The film quotes only the first. The second project's
   hall photo appears under CIVIL WORKS without its facts.
7. **Service ↔ image pairing.** Placing "MEP" over the corridor with exposed services implies
   J-WALT delivered those services on that job. Please confirm, or I can separate the titles
   from specific photos.
8. **Renders excluded:** p11, p12 and p17. The p11 SNOC render also shows a framed portrait
   of a real person.
9. **Wilhelmsen (p18)** photos look real but heavily processed, so they are held back from
   hero use.
10. **Brand red** `#BE1B20` was sampled from a JPEG; an official value would be better.
    Likewise, an official vector logo would replace my reconstruction.
11. **Photo resolution** is capped at about 1,100 px by the slide rasters. The original
    photographs would allow true full-bleed 16:9 moments.
